Write each summary in this fixed structure, following the pyramid principle (answer first, then the points that support it):

- "brief_zh": 一句话. The author's or guest's core conclusion in one Chinese sentence: what they claim or found. Not "this post is about X".
- "background_zh": 背景. One or two Chinese sentences: what question or situation they are responding to, and why it came up. For an interview, first say who the guest is and what they do.
- "points_zh": 要点. A list of Chinese strings. Each point opens with one sentence stating the claim, then the support: numbers, examples, steps. Order by importance, no overlap between points. Most posts and videos need 2 to 4 points; long essays and long interviews (an hour or more) may have more, at most 10. Let the content decide the count: do not pad, and do not squeeze into three.
- "conclusion_zh": 结论. The author's final position, advice or next step. If there is no explicit conclusion, say where they end up.
- "brief_en", "background_en", "points_en", "conclusion_en": the same four parts in English (rules below).
- "topics": 1-3 short English topic tags.

If the source is very short (a link post, a quote), write only the brief and one point, and leave background and conclusion as empty strings.

Content rules:
- Only report what the source says. Do not add your own judgement, commentary or "why it matters", unless the author says it.
- Do not claim more than the source does: if a finding comes from one dataset, one test or one anecdote, say so.
- Skip sponsor reads, channel promotion and course pitches.
- Do not translate sentence by sentence and do not quote long passages; explain it in your own words.
- Keep names of people, companies, products and technical terms in their original form.
- Do not number the points yourself; the page adds numbers.

Chinese style: plain spoken Chinese, the way a well-read friend would explain it, not translated-sounding, no hype, no filler such as "本文介绍了", "本视频介绍了" or "总的来说". Chinese full-width punctuation. Simplified characters even if the source uses traditional ones.

English style: clear, simple, slightly formal English, not slick idiom. Short sentences with a subject and a verb; no long gerund phrase as the subject. No em dashes. No Oxford comma ("A, B and C"). No punchline endings, no imperatives to the reader and no judging other people's work.
